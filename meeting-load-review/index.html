Review meeting load without guessing from hours alone

The calendar shows scheduled meetings. Work context adds another perspective on what happened around them. This review connects purpose and placement to a specific task instead of treating every meeting as the same cost.

By Manuel Castillejo · 2026-10-11

In this guide
  • List the purpose of each recurring meeting
  • Look at placement
  • Choose one adjustment
  • Review usefulness and cost

List the purpose of each recurring meeting

For each meeting, state the decision or coordination it supports. Note the necessary participants, preparation and follow-up. If the purpose cannot be explained, that is a question for the owner rather than proof that the event was useless.

Collect a concrete outcome from recent instances. A meeting may prevent duplicated work or resolve uncertainty that would otherwise continue. Assess that contribution alongside time, not solely through duration.

Look at placement

Two meetings grouped together can leave a continuous work period afterward. The same meetings spaced apart can divide a morning into short fragments. Consider the requirements of a particular task and compare the calendar with FlowSight's reviewable work context.

Avoid inferring causation from proximity. Workload, urgent support and technical blockers may also change progress. Describe the observed schedule and your interpretation separately, with a concrete example of work that lacked suitable space.

Choose one adjustment

Options include grouping an event with another, shortening a routine update, changing attendees or moving a status update to a written format. Define the decision the new process must preserve. Removing a meeting without replacing needed coordination can move work into fragmented conversations.

Agree on urgent-response expectations and a trial period. Protecting calendar space alone may not help if requests still arrive throughout it. Explain the full agreement so that participants understand both the schedule and how exceptions are handled.

Review usefulness and cost

Use the worksheet to compare purpose, placement and outcome before and after the adjustment. Ask participants whether the new arrangement supports their work. Look at actual task outcomes as well as available time.

Keep the conclusion local to the trial. A useful change for one team does not establish that all meetings should follow the same rule. Retain what helped, change what failed and set a review date when the team's work or responsibilities evolve.
