Questions to answer before a FlowSight team pilot

A team pilot should test a defined workflow and a useful decision. Agree on purpose and information boundaries before asking people to contribute context. These questions help keep the trial understandable.

By Manuel Castillejo · 2026-10-11

In this guide
  • Which decision are you improving?
  • What information is necessary?
  • Who reviews the process?
  • When do you decide what to keep?

Which decision are you improving?

Choose a concrete purpose: reconsider recurring meetings, reduce duplicated reporting or understand capacity for one project. Explain why the current process is insufficient and what a useful improvement would look like. A broad desire for visibility leaves too many secondary uses undefined.

Specify which work and participants are in scope. Different responsibilities can produce different activity patterns. A pilot designed for one group should not become an individual ranking system or a claim about every role.

What information is necessary?

FlowSight describes local analysis of raw screen context and optional sharing of selected summaries and aggregate signals. Identify the exact features the pilot will enable. Review connected services separately, including any external AI or integration that can receive report data.

Define the minimum information needed, its recipients and retention. The original analysis being local does not resolve every privacy obligation of shared information. A share option does not automatically establish the organization's lawful basis or appropriate purpose.

Who reviews the process?

Involve the responsible privacy function and qualified advice where appropriate before an employment-context deployment. Explain the system to affected people, including what it omits and what it must not be used to infer. Required consultation or participation depends on the setting.

Review outputs with the people doing the work. Their context can explain support responsibilities, inaccessible periods and contributions the activity interpretation misses. A useful team discussion considers working conditions and outcomes together.

When do you decide what to keep?

Give the pilot a review date. Record which decisions changed, what feedback participants provided and whether unnecessary data were shared. Check the cost of the process as well as any benefit. A new reporting ritual can add work even when its summaries are readable.

Decide to retain, revise or stop specific capabilities. Record the reason and limits of the evidence. Check current product conditions and plan configuration on the official site; this guide is a process resource, not a promise about commercial availability.
